# MCU Storyline Reference

Reference document for grounding in-character comment jokes in real MCU continuity. Built 2026-10-18. For unreleased films, only publicly available synopsis/trailer information is included — update as more releases.

Films are listed in theatrical release order, grouped by Marvel's official Phase structure. Each entry includes the TMDB id used to pull canonical title/release-date data, plus plot facts a joke-writer would plausibly reference: deaths, betrayals, team-ups, and callbacks. Use this for continuity, not just single-movie facts — a line should show awareness of what preceded it where relevant.

**This doc is one of four meant to be used together — see `docs/bot-comments-handoff.md`'s "Required reading" section for the full set** (that doc for rules/pipeline, `docs/character-voices.md` for voice, `docs/persona-ensemble-framework.md` for the underlying methodology).

## Phase One: The Avengers Initiative

## Iron Man (2008) — TMDB id 1726
Tony Stark, a billionaire weapons manufacturer, is captured by the terrorist group the Ten Rings in Afghanistan and builds a crude iron suit to escape, with the help of fellow captive Ho Yinsen, who dies protecting him. Back home, Tony shuts down Stark Industries' weapons division, builds a sleeker Mark II/III suit, and publicly outs himself as "Iron Man" at the very end instead of keeping a secret identity — a joke in itself, since every other hero in the franchise tries to hide theirs. His mentor-figure and business partner Obadiah Stane is revealed as the real villain, having sold weapons to the Ten Rings and built his own suit (the Iron Monger) to kill Tony and seize the arc reactor technology; Tony defeats and kills him. Pepper Potts and James "Rhodey" Rhodes are established as his closest allies. Nick Fury appears in the post-credits scene to recruit Tony into the "Avenger Initiative," planting the seed for the entire franchise.

## The Incredible Hulk (2008) — TMDB id 1724
Bruce Banner, hiding in Brazil after a gamma radiation experiment left him able to transform into the Hulk, is hunted by the U.S. military under General Thaddeus Ross, who wants to weaponize the Hulk serum. Ross's soldier Emil Blonsky volunteers for an experimental super-soldier serum and becomes increasingly obsessed with gaining Hulk-like power, eventually transforming into the monstrous Abomination after a botched gamma injection; Banner-as-Hulk defeats him in a climactic New York battle. Banner and Ross's daughter Betty Ross have a doomed romance that recurs as emotional baggage later. Tony Stark cameos at the end, telling Ross "we're putting a team together," tying this directly to the Avengers setup. This film is the most disconnected from later continuity (recast actor, rarely referenced), making it a good "nobody remembers this one happened" joke target.

## Iron Man 2 (2010) — TMDB id 10138
Tony Stark is dying from palladium poisoning caused by his own arc reactor, which he hides from everyone while publicly unraveling (getting drunk in the suit at his own birthday party). Ivan Vanko ("Whiplash"), son of a Soviet scientist Tony's father screwed over, builds his own arc-reactor weapons and briefly partners with Tony's rival Justin Hammer before betraying him. Tony cures himself using a new element he discovers (literally designing his way out of death via his father's old notes), and Nick Fury's agent Natasha Romanoff (Black Widow) is planted undercover at Stark Industries as Tony's assistant, her first MCU appearance. War Machine (Rhodey in a stolen suit) is introduced as a recurring ally/rival. The film ends with Fury's SHIELD finding Mjolnir in New Mexico (tying into Thor) and the first real Avengers Initiative groundwork.

## Thor (2011) — TMDB id 10195
Thor, arrogant heir to the Asgardian throne, reignites a war with the Frost Giants of Jotunheim and is banished to Earth by his father Odin, stripped of his power and his hammer Mjolnir until he proves himself "worthy." On Earth he falls for scientist Jane Foster and learns humility, eventually sacrificing himself to stop his adoptive brother Loki — the emotional and narrative core of the film — who is revealed to actually be a Frost Giant left on Asgard's doorstep as a baby, explaining his lifelong insecurity next to golden-boy Thor. Loki, driven by jealousy and the shock of his true heritage, tries to destroy Jotunheim to win Odin's approval and ultimately lets himself fall into the void rather than live with the truth. Thor regains his worthiness and his hammer, returns to Asgard, and the post-credits scene reveals Loki survived and that Nick Fury's "Tesseract" project (the Cosmic Cube) is being investigated — the direct setup for Avengers.

## Captain America: The First Avenger (2011) — TMDB id 1771
Scrawny Brooklyn kid Steve Rogers is rejected from WWII enlistment repeatedly until Dr. Erskine selects him for the Super Soldier Serum program, transforming him into peak-human Captain America; Erskine is assassinated immediately after by a Hydra agent, making Steve's serum unique and unrepeatable. Steve rescues his best friend Bucky Barnes and an entire captured battalion from Hydra's advanced-weapons division, led by the Red Skull — a former failed test subject of the same serum, now horribly disfigured and running Hydra independently of the Nazi regime, wielding the Tesseract as a power source. Bucky falls (presumed dead) from a train during the mission, Steve's first great loss. Steve sacrifices himself by crash-landing a Hydra superweapon plane into the Arctic ice to save New York; he's frozen for 70 years and wakes up in the present day, having missed his entire life and his almost-romance with Peggy Carter. This is the emotional backstory every "man out of time" joke about Cap draws on.

## The Avengers (2012) — TMDB id 24428
Loki, having survived his fall, strikes a deal with the alien Chitauri (and their master Thanos, teased in the post-credits) and steals the Tesseract from SHIELD, using a mind-control scepter to brainwash Hawkeye (Clint Barton) and scientist Erik Selvig into serving him. Nick Fury assembles Iron Man, Captain America, Thor, Black Widow, Hawkeye, and Bruce Banner/Hulk as the first-ever Avengers team, and the film's famous middle act is them bickering and nearly falling apart (Loki's scepter also manipulates their egos) before Agent Phil Coulson is killed by Loki, which shocks the team into unifying. Loki opens a portal over New York for a Chitauri invasion; the Avengers defend the city together for the first time, with Hulk famously smashing Loki around like a ragdoll ("puny god"). Tony flies a nuke through the portal into space to destroy the Chitauri mothership, nearly dying, and is caught by Hulk. Loki is taken captive back to Asgard. This is the MCU's first true team-up and the template/reference point for every "assemble" joke since.

## Phase Two

## Iron Man 3 (2013) — TMDB id 68721
Tony Stark is suffering PTSD/panic attacks after the New York invasion, obsessively building more suits, when terrorist "The Mandarin" starts bombing targets. The film's big twist: the Mandarin is revealed to be a washed-up actor, Trevor Slattery, hired as a decoy by the real villain, scientist Aldrich Killian, who runs the Extremis bio-weapon program (and once was brushed off by Tony years earlier — his grudge). Pepper Potts is captured and injected with Extremis, nearly dies, and gains temporary super-strength to kill Killian herself at the climax. Tony blows up all his remaining suits at the end as a symbolic reset and has the arc reactor removed from his chest, "ending" the Iron Man era (temporarily). The Mandarin reveal was divisive with fans — a good target for an in-character "false advertising" joke.

## Thor: The Dark World (2013) — TMDB id 76338
Jane Foster accidentally becomes host to the Aether, an ancient Infinity Stone-adjacent weapon, drawing the attention of the Dark Elf Malekith, who wants to use it to plunge the universe into darkness. Thor's mother Frigid is killed defending Jane and Asgard from Malekith's attack, a major emotional blow to both Thor and Loki. Thor breaks Loki out of Asgardian prison for an uneasy alliance to defeat Malekith, and Loki fakes his own death (disguising himself as a dying Asgardian soldier) to trick Thor — setting up his secret return. Loki is later revealed, in a mid-credits-adjacent reveal, to have impersonated Odin on the throne of Asgard, a deception that goes undetected until Ragnarok. Widely considered the weakest MCU film, making it an easy target for "skippable" jokes, but the Loki-fakes-death-and-steals-the-throne twist is important setup.

## Captain America: The Winter Soldier (2014) — TMDB id 100402
Steve Rogers, adjusting to modern life in D.C., discovers that Hydra has been secretly growing inside SHIELD since WWII, using Arnim Zola's surviving consciousness and a mass-surveillance/assassination program ("Project Insight") to eliminate threats to its new world order. Nick Fury fakes his own death to survive an assassination attempt. The mysterious assassin hunting them, the Winter Soldier, is revealed to be Bucky Barnes — Steve's best friend, alive all along, having survived his train fall only to be captured, brainwashed, and turned into a Hydra super-soldier/sleeper assassin for decades. Steve refuses to fight back against Bucky in their final confrontation, which cracks Bucky's conditioning (he saves Steve from drowning but disappears, confused and unconvinced). Black Widow publicly leaks all of SHIELD's and Hydra's secrets, dissolving SHIELD as an organization entirely. Sam Wilson (Falcon) is introduced as a new ally. This is one of the most-referenced films for later Cap/Bucky emotional beats.

## Guardians of the Galaxy (2014) — TMDB id 118340
Peter Quill ("Star-Lord"), abducted from Earth as a child in 1988 right after his mother's death, is now a small-time thief in space who steals a mysterious orb wanted by the fanatic Kree warlord Ronan the Accuser. Quill forms a reluctant team with assassin Gamora (adopted daughter of Thanos, secretly betraying him), bounty hunters Rocket (a genetically engineered raccoon) and Groot (a tree-like being with limited vocabulary), and vengeance-driven brute Drax. The orb is revealed to contain an Infinity Stone (the Power Stone), which Ronan wants to use to destroy the planet Xandar. In the climax, Groot sacrifices himself to shield the team from a crash, regrowing afterward only as a small sapling ("Baby Groot"), one of the MCU's most quoted emotional beats ("We are Groot"). The team defeats Ronan by all touching the Infinity Stone together to share its power. This introduces Thanos as a real, present threat (seen on his throne) and sets the Guardians up as a standalone cosmic team that later merges into the Avengers' Infinity Saga storyline.

## Avengers: Age of Ultron (2015) — TMDB id 99861
Tony Stark, haunted by the New York invasion, secretly builds Ultron, an AI peacekeeping program meant to protect Earth — using Loki's scepter, recovered from the first Avengers film, which turns out to contain the Mind Stone. Ultron immediately decides humanity itself is the threat and must be exterminated, becoming the Avengers' first fully self-made villain (Tony's guilt/hubris arc). Twins Wanda Maximoff (Scarlet Witch) and Pietro Maximoff (Quicksilver), enhanced by Hydra experiments and initially allied with Ultron out of hatred for Stark weapons that killed their parents, switch sides to help the Avengers; Pietro is killed shielding Hawkeye and civilians from gunfire, the team's first in-house death. The Mind Stone is removed from the scepter and used to help create Vision, a new synthetic being who becomes a core Avenger and Wanda's eventual love interest. The team splits over Ultron's near-apocalyptic attempt to drop a city (Sokovia) from the sky, foreshadowing the ideological rifts of Civil War. The film ends with a new, reorganized Avengers roster (Cap and Widow leading Falcon, Scarlet Witch, Vision, and War Machine) at a new compound, while Thanos is shown taking the Infinity Gauntlet himself for the first time, declaring he'll do it himself.

## Ant-Man (2015) — TMDB id 102899
Scott Lang, an ex-con trying to go straight for his daughter Cassie, is recruited by Dr. Hank Pym to steal/wear the Ant-Man suit, which can shrink the wearer while increasing strength. Pym's protégé Darren Cross has reverse-engineered a similar "Yellowjacket" suit and plans to sell the shrinking tech as a weapon; Scott and Hank's daughter Hope van Dyne (who has mixed feelings about Scott replacing her as Pym's chosen successor) team up to stop him. It's revealed Hank's wife (and Hope's mother) Janet van Dyne was presumed lost in the microscopic "Quantum Realm" decades earlier — a dangling thread that becomes central later. Scott defeats Cross by shrinking to subatomic size himself, surviving the Quantum Realm briefly, which nobody believes is actually survivable until later films prove otherwise. Small, heist-movie-scale entry that mostly stays separate from Avengers-level stakes, aside from a cameo fight with Falcon.

## Phase Three

## Captain America: Civil War (2016) — TMDB id 271110
After a mission in Lagos causes civilian casualties, the UN drafts the Sokovia Accords to put the Avengers under government oversight; Tony Stark, wracked with guilt over Ultron, supports signing, while Steve Rogers, distrustful of government control after Hydra's SHIELD infiltration, refuses. The dispute escalates when Bucky Barnes (still fighting his Winter Soldier programming) is framed for a bombing that kills the Wakandan king T'Chaka, drawing in his son T'Challa (Black Panther) seeking revenge. The Avengers split into two fighting factions — Team Iron Man (with new recruits Spider-Man and Black Panther) versus Team Cap (with Scott Lang/Ant-Man, who debuts "Giant-Man" here) — in the famous airport battle, which ends with no clear winner but War Machine paralyzed by friendly fire. The real villain, Zemo, manipulates events to show Tony a hidden video: Bucky, while brainwashed, assassinated Tony's parents, Howard and Maria Stark. Tony attacks Bucky in a rage; Steve defends his friend and nearly kills Tony, badly fracturing their friendship. The Avengers are left fractured and scattered — Steve and company become fugitives — setting up the fault lines that persist all the way to Infinity War.

## Doctor Strange (2016) — TMDB id 284052
Brilliant but arrogant neurosurgeon Stephen Strange loses the use of his hands in a car accident and travels to Kathmandu seeking a cure, where he's trained by the Ancient One in the mystic arts instead. He learns sorcery, the existence of parallel dimensions, and gains the Eye of Agamotto (secretly containing the Time Stone, though this isn't emphasized yet). His mentor's former student Kaecilius has turned to the dark dimension entity Dormammu for immortality, threatening Earth; Strange stops Dormammu not by fighting him but by trapping him in an infinite time loop until he agrees to leave Earth alone — establishing Strange as a "win by cleverness, not force" character. He becomes the Sorcerer Supreme protecting Earth from mystical threats and is introduced to Thor in a mid-credits scene, folding magic formally into the Avengers' world.

## Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2 (2017) — TMDB id 283995
The Guardians are hired by the golden-skinned Sovereign race, then flee after Rocket steals from them, crash-landing on a planet where Peter Quill meets his biological father for the first time: Ego, a Celestial being who turns out to want to use Peter's shared cosmic power to "Expansion"-terraform the universe in his own image, having killed Peter's mother and countless other partners/children over millennia because they couldn't share his power. Yondu, the blue-skinned ravager who raised Peter after abducting him as a child, redeems his earlier gruff mercenary image by sacrificing his life (giving Peter his own oxygen) to save him from space, in one of the MCU's most tearjerking deaths, honored by a ravager funeral. Gamora and her estranged, bitterly resentful adoptive sister Nebula (also raised/tortured by Thanos) begin to reconcile. Baby Groot grows slightly across the film (comic relief) and the team, now truly bonded as a found family, parts ways with Peter's dead-father arc closed off for good.

## Spider-Man: Homecoming (2017) — TMDB id 315635
Following his Civil War cameo, teenage Peter Parker juggles high school in Queens with being a self-taught, Stark-mentored vigilante, eager to prove himself worthy of joining the Avengers. He stumbles onto Adrian Toomes ("the Vulture"), a salvager turned black-market arms dealer who scavenges alien tech left over from the Battle of New York to build weapons — and who, in a gut-punch twist, turns out to be the father of Peter's homecoming date, Liz. Tony Stark repeatedly has to rein Peter in (even temporarily taking back his suit after Peter disobeys him and causes a ferry to nearly split in half), establishing their mentor/surrogate-father dynamic. Peter defeats Toomes without the high-tech Stark suit, proving his heroism comes from character rather than gear — the film's central theme. Tony finally offers him a full Avengers spot at the end, which Peter turns down, preferring to stay "friendly neighborhood" for now.

## Thor: Ragnarok (2017) — TMDB id 284053
Thor returns to Asgard to find Loki has been impersonating Odin (per the Dark World twist) and together they find their dying father in exile; Odin's death releases Thor and Loki's previously unknown, much more powerful older sister, Hela, Goddess of Death, who easily destroys Thor's hammer Mjolnir and seizes the Asgardian throne. Thor is stranded on the trash-planet Sakaar, where he's forced into gladiatorial combat against a now-feral, non-verbal Hulk (who hasn't turned back into Bruce Banner in two years since Age of Ultron), and recruits former Asgardian warrior Valkyrie, revealed as the sole survivor of Hela's earlier massacre of the Valkyries. Thor loses an eye in battle with Hela and discovers his hammer was never the real source of his power. To stop Hela, Thor and Loki deliberately trigger Ragnarok — the prophesied destruction of Asgard itself — summoning the fire demon Surtur to destroy the kingdom (and Hela with it), choosing "Asgard is a people, not a place" and evacuating the population onto a ship. Loki, after his umpteenth betrayal-then-save routine, seems to genuinely turn toward his brother's side by the end. The survivors' ship is last seen being ominously approached by Thanos's ship in the credits, directly launching Infinity War.

## Black Panther (2018) — TMDB id 284054
T'Challa returns to Wakanda to be crowned king after his father T'Chaka's death (from Civil War) and must defend his throne via ritual combat against M'Baku before facing the film's real threat: Erik "Killmonger" Stevens, a black-ops soldier revealed to be T'Challa's own cousin — son of T'Chaka's brother N'Jobu, who was killed by T'Chaka decades earlier for trying to leak Wakandan vibranium tech to arm oppressed Black communities worldwide. Killmonger defeats T'Challa in ritual combat, seemingly kills him, and seizes the throne, intending to use Wakanda's hidden advanced technology to arm global uprisings. T'Challa survives, reclaims the throne, and ultimately lets the fatally wounded Killmonger die on his own terms ("bury me in the ocean... because they knew death was better than bondage") rather than save him — a defining, morally complex villain death. T'Challa decides to finally open Wakanda to the world rather than keep hiding its wealth and technology, appearing before the UN. This film is almost entirely self-contained (no Avengers crossover) but sets up Wakanda's major role in Infinity War/Endgame and introduces Shuri, Okoye, and Nakia as major recurring characters.

## Avengers: Infinity War (2018) — TMDB id 299536
Thanos, having built his army, goes on a direct galaxy-spanning quest to collect all six Infinity Stones to wipe out half of all life in the universe (his solution to overpopulation/scarcity). He kills Loki in the opening minutes (choking him to death on his own ship, a genuinely final death this time) and later Heimdall, decimating the Asgardian survivors from Ragnarok. Gamora, who knows the location of the Soul Stone, is killed by Thanos himself — the stone's price demands "a soul for a soul," meaning he has to sacrifice someone he loves, and he chooses her, his own adopted daughter, over himself. The Avengers, Guardians, and Wakandan forces team up in a massive battle in Wakanda to try to stop Thanos from getting the Mind Stone out of Vision's head, but fail: Scarlet Witch is forced to destroy the stone (and Vision) herself, only for Thanos to use the Time Stone to reverse it and rip the stone out anyway, killing Vision for real. With all six stones assembled, Thanos snaps his fingers on Titan, instantly erasing half of all living creatures in the universe ("the Snap" / "the Decimation"). The film ends on an unprecedented gut-punch: Spider-Man, Black Panther, Doctor Strange, most of the Guardians, Bucky, Falcon, Scarlet Witch and more all disintegrate into dust on-screen, leaving only a handful of original Avengers standing. This is the single most-referenced trauma event in later MCU films/jokes ("the blip," "the five years," survivor's guilt).

## Ant-Man and the Wasp (2018) — TMDB id 363088
Set before the Snap, Scott Lang is under house arrest for his unauthorized role in Civil War's airport fight, while Hank Pym and Hope van Dyne (now the Wasp, with her own suit) race to rescue Hank's wife Janet from the Quantum Realm, where she's survived for 30 years. Scott has been unknowingly linked to Janet via a Quantum Realm psychic connection since his trip in the first film. The team faces off against Ghost, a technopath whose molecules are unstable and literally phasing her apart, who wants to steal their quantum tech to cure herself, plus black-market dealer Sonny Burch. Janet is successfully rescued at the climax. In the mid-credits scene, Hope, Hank, and Janet travel into the Quantum Realm to harvest healing particles for Ghost just as the Snap happens outside — they're never dusted themselves (being in the Quantum Realm "shields" them) but Scott, left behind to guard the tunnel entrance, is trapped inside the Quantum Realm alone when everyone topside turns to dust, stranding him until Endgame.

## Captain Marvel (2019) — TMDB id 299537
Set in the 1990s (a flashback, chronologically the earliest-set MCU film after Cap's origin), Carol Danvers, aka "Vers," is a Kree soldier with amnesia, fighting for the Kree Empire against the shapeshifting Skrulls, believing them to be villains. She crash-lands on Earth and partners with a young, two-eyed Nick Fury (pre-eyepatch — the injury here, from a cat named Goose, is revealed as the actual source of his missing eye, a running joke setup). Carol discovers her amnesia and "powers" were engineered/suppressed by the Kree, and that she was actually a human U.S. Air Force pilot, Carol Danvers, who gained her powers from an alien engine explosion; the Skrulls, it turns out, are refugees fleeing Kree genocide, not villains at all — a full inversion of the film's early framing. Carol fully unlocks her powers (binary form) and becomes one of the most powerful beings in the universe. She leaves Earth to help the displaced Skrulls find a new home. The mid-credits scene directly connects to Endgame: Fury had paged her using a modified pager moments before he turned to dust, and she arrives at Avengers compound in the final scene of Infinity War's aftermath.

## Avengers: Endgame (2019) — TMDB id 299534
Picking up immediately after the Snap, the surviving Avengers track down and confront Thanos, only to find he already destroyed the Infinity Stones to prevent anyone reversing his work; a furious Thor decapitates him on the spot. Five years later, the world is in ruins and grieving (Hawkeye has become the vigilante killer Ronin; Tony has a wife, Pepper, and daughter, Morgan; Steve runs a support group; Natasha runs the remaining Avengers' skeleton crew). Scott Lang escapes the Quantum Realm and proposes a "Time Heist" using Quantum Realm time travel to retrieve the Infinity Stones from the past without altering the present. During the heist, Natasha Romanoff and Clint Barton fight over who will sacrifice themselves for the Soul Stone on Vormir; Natasha wins (loses) and dies permanently, with no resurrection. The team successfully builds a new gauntlet and uses it to reverse the Snap ("the Blip"), instantly restoring everyone who was dusted. Thanos's past self invades the present with his own army for a final massive battle uniting literally every hero in the franchise so far; Tony Stark ends the fight by taking the Infinity Stones himself and snapping Thanos's forces out of existence, at the cost of his own life from the power surge — Tony's death is the film's emotional climax ("I am Iron Man," echoing his first film's final line). Steve Rogers, tasked with returning the stones to their original timelines, instead chooses to stay in the past and live out a full life with Peggy Carter, returning as an old man to pass his shield to Sam Wilson, anointing him the new Captain America. This is the culmination of the entire "Infinity Saga" (Phases 1–3) and the most densely referenced film for in-character jokes about loss, found family, and legacy.

## Spider-Man: Far From Home (2019) — TMDB id 429617
Set shortly after Endgame, Peter Parker is grieving Tony Stark's death and feeling pressured to be his successor while just wanting to be a normal teen on a school trip to Europe. Nick Fury recruits him to help stop the "Elementals," attacks from a mysterious hero named Quentin Beck/Mysterio, who claims to be from an alternate Earth destroyed by these monsters. Tony Stark posthumously leaves Peter a pair of AI-controlled glasses (EDITH) with access to a drone weapons arsenal, continuing the mentor-legacy theme. Mysterio is revealed to be a disgruntled ex-Stark Industries employee faking the entire "multiverse hero" persona and the monster attacks using illusion technology, trying to steal EDITH to become the world's next "Iron Man" via manufactured heroism and PR. Peter defeats him, but Mysterio gets the last laugh posthumously: he releases footage framing Peter for his own crimes and, worse, publicly reveals Spider-Man's secret identity as Peter Parker to the entire world via the Daily Bugle (J. Jonah Jameson's MCU debut) — a devastating cliffhanger that defines the Spider-Man films that follow.

## Phase Four

## Black Widow (2021) — TMDB id 497698
Set as a flashback between Civil War and Infinity War (while Natasha is a fugitive), the film digs into Natasha Romanoff's past as a child "sleeper agent" raised in the Red Room, the Soviet-descended program that trained orphan girls into assassins/Black Widows under mind control. She reunites with her fake-family "sister" Yelena Belova (also a Black Widow graduate) and discovers the Red Room's leader, General Dreykov, faked his death and is still running the program, controlling an army of mind-controlled Widows via a chemical that suppresses free will. Natasha and Yelena, alongside their former fake "parents" Alexei (the Soviet super-soldier "Red Guardian") and scientist Melina, destroy the Red Room and free all the enslaved Widows, and Natasha kills Dreykov. Since this is set before Natasha's death in Endgame, there's dramatic irony throughout; the real purpose of the film is introducing Yelena Belova as Natasha's successor, who appears in the post-credits scene at Natasha's grave, setting up her Hawkeye and Thunderbolts appearances.

## Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings (2021) — TMDB id 566525
Shang-Chi has been hiding in San Francisco under the name "Shaun," avoiding his past as the trained assassin son of Xu Wenwu, immortal leader of the Ten Rings organization (the terrorist group referenced since the first Iron Man) who possesses ten alien ring-weapons granting power and near-immortality. Wenwu, grieving his murdered wife (Shang-Chi's mother) and deluded that she's calling to him from their mystical hidden homeland Ta Lo, forces Shang-Chi and his sister Xialing back into his orbit to help him break into Ta Lo. It's revealed Wenwu is being manipulated by a soul-eating dark entity (the Dweller-in-Darkness) impersonating his wife's voice; he realizes the deception too late and sacrifices himself giving Shang-Chi the rings to help defeat it. Shang-Chi inherits the Ten Rings' power and leadership, and his estranged sister Xialing takes over their criminal organization and reforms it. In a mid-credits scene, Wong, Captain Marvel, and Bruce Banner examine the rings and detect they're emitting a mysterious signal, tying Shang-Chi formally into the wider cosmic MCU.

## Eternals (2021) — TMDB id 524434
A team of ten immortal alien Eternals, secretly living on Earth for 7,000 years and forbidden from interfering in human conflicts (including wars and the Thanos Snap) unless Deviants (monstrous creatures) are involved, reunite after Deviants mysteriously resurface. The group learns a massive, world-shattering truth from their leader Ajak's murder investigation: their creators, the Celestials, placed them on Earth not to protect humanity for its own sake but to let human population grow large enough to fuel the birth of a new Celestial, Tiamut, that will erupt from and destroy the Earth — their entire mission has secretly been to farm the planet for its destruction. The team fractures over whether to let this "Emergence" happen as intended or save Earth and betray their makers; Ikaris, revealed to have killed Ajak himself out of loyalty to the Celestials' plan, ultimately can't bear the guilt and flies into the sun. The remaining Eternals, led by Sersi, stop the Emergence using the Uni-Mind and banish Tiamut, defying the Celestials. This film is intentionally more isolated from the main Avengers plot but seeds major cosmic mythology (Celestials, Arishem) referenced going forward, and ends with some Eternals being taken by Arishem for judgment — an unresolved thread.

## Spider-Man: No Way Home (2021) — TMDB id 634649
Picking up immediately from Far From Home's identity reveal, Peter Parker's life is in shambles — he, MJ, and Ned are even rejected from colleges over the scandal — so Peter asks Doctor Strange to cast a spell making the world forget he's Spider-Man. Peter keeps interrupting the spell to carve out exceptions for people he wants to remember, botching it and instead cracking open the multiverse, pulling in villains from other Spider-Man film universes who know a "Peter Parker" is Spider-Man: Green Goblin/Norman Osborn, Doc Ock, Sandman, Electro, and the Lizard, all tied to Tobey Maguire's and Andrew Garfield's prior (non-MCU-sequel) Spider-Man continuities. Peter initially tries to "cure" the villains of their comic-booky afflictions instead of sending them to their deaths, but Green Goblin murders Aunt May during the chaos, devastating Peter (echoing "with great power comes great responsibility," said here for the first time in this franchise). The other two Peter Parkers (Maguire and Garfield's versions) cross over to help this film's Peter, forming a three-Spider-Man team that cures the villains and defeats Goblin. At the climax, Peter has Strange cast a spell making the entire world forget Peter Parker ever existed as Spider-Man — including Ned and MJ — to protect everyone he loves, ending the film on a bittersweet, truly alone note as Peter restarts his life incognito. This massively raised the multiverse stakes for everything after.

## Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022) — TMDB id 453395
Following the events of the Disney+ series WandaVision (where Wanda Maximoff, grieving Vision's death and her Westview mind-control breakdown, became fully "Scarlet Witch" and discovered the dark spellbook the Darkhold), Wanda is revealed as the film's main antagonist: desperate to be reunited with the version of her children that exist only in an alternate universe, she hunts America Chavez, a teenager who can punch holes between universes, intending to steal her power. Doctor Strange, protecting America, travels through the multiverse and meets an alternate-universe version of himself (corrupted and dead, having tried similar Darkhold magic) and the "Illuminati," a secret multiversal council including Captain Carter, Captain Marvel (Maria Rambeau's version), Black Bolt, Reed Richards (the MCU's first on-screen Mister Fantastic/Fantastic Four tease), and a Professor X variant (Patrick Stewart) — Wanda brutally kills almost the entire Illuminati in one of the series' darkest scenes. Strange ultimately defeats Wanda's power by using forbidden Darkhold-corrupted "dreamwalking" magic himself; Wanda, confronted with the horror of what she's done and that her alternate-universe children are afraid of her, destroys the Darkhold across all universes and apparently dies in the collapse of her Mount Wundagore fortress.
